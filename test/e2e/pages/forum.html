How should I flatten this inherited plane sole?

Rowan asks

I inherited a century-old smoothing plane whose iron is sharp but whose sole rocks slightly on a reference plate. I want to use it for furniture rather than polish away every mark. What is the least aggressive way to make it reliable?

Mira replies

First check the sole with the lever cap tightened, because body tension can change the reading. Mark the high spots with a permanent marker, then take only a few strokes over abrasive paper fixed to thick glass.

Jon replies

Do not chase a mirror finish. The toe, the area immediately ahead of the mouth, and the heel need to share a plane; shallow hollows elsewhere will not prevent accurate work and may preserve useful evidence of age.

Rowan follows up

The three bearing areas now register evenly after six careful passes. On a walnut test board the plane produced a continuous shaving, so I stopped there and cleaned the adjustment threads before putting it back to work.
