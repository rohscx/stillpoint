A working harbor makes room for marshland

At sunrise on Tuesday, engineers opened the final gate beside Bellweather Harbor, allowing the tide to reach wetlands cut off from the sea for almost seventy years. The first water moved slowly through the channel, carrying silver fish between newly planted reeds.

The restoration is designed to protect nearby homes from storm surges while rebuilding habitat for herons, oysters, and migrating shorebirds. Crews removed obsolete concrete walls but retained the stone pier used by the town's fishing fleet.

Marine biologist Lena Ortiz said the project treats the waterfront as both essential infrastructure and a living ecosystem. Sensors will measure salinity, water height, and sediment through every season for the next five years.

Local fishers supported the revised plan after designers widened the navigation lane. The harbor remains open throughout the work, and the public path will reopen once safety railings are installed this autumn.
