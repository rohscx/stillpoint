Repairing the notebook that crossed three mountain ranges

My oldest field notebook arrived home with a broken spine, rain-softened corners, and red dust worked into every seam. Replacing it would have been easy, but the pencilled observations inside belonged with the wear gathered during the journey.

I began by lifting loose threads with a blunt needle and brushing grit away from the folded sections. A narrow strip of Japanese tissue restored the hinge without making the cover rigid, which matters when the book must open flat on a rock.

The new linen thread follows the original sewing stations. I tightened each pass only enough to hold the paper together, because excessive tension can tear damp sheets months after a repair appears finished.

Now the notebook opens quietly and the repaired spine flexes with the pages. The stains remain, as they should; conservation is most convincing when it supports an object's history instead of pretending that history never happened.
